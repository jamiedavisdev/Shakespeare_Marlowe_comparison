# Are Marlowe's plays and early Shakespeare by the same hand? First results

Date: 2026-10-30. Corpus and methods are described in README.md; every number below is reproducible
from the scripts and the tables in `results/`.

## Bottom line

The evidence does not support a single author. Three independent measurements agree:

1. **Marlowe's plays recognise each other.** A character-level model trained on Marlowe's other plays
   predicts a held-out Marlowe play better than any Shakespeare model does, for 6 of 7 plays
   (`results/crossentropy_masked/affinity.md`). The stylometric nearest-centroid test gets 5 of 7 right,
   the misses going to Kyd, never to Shakespeare.
2. **Shakespeare's non-Henry VI early plays are as foreign to Marlowe as a known different author.**
   For Titus, Richard III, Shrew, Two Gentlemen and Errors, the Marlowe model is 0.07-0.10 bits/char
   worse than the Shakespeare model. Fletcher's plays, which Marlowe certainly did not write, score
   0.04-0.09. Later Shakespeare scores 0.09-0.13. A chunk classifier (function words, leave-one-play-out)
   separates Marlowe from these five plays with accuracy 0.85 / AUC 0.92, the same as it separates
   Shakespeare from Fletcher (0.93 / 0.89-0.94).
3. **The one Marlowe-like group is Henry VI 1-3, and Kyd is equally Marlowe-like.** With the trilogy,
   Richard III and proper nouns removed from training, the Marlowe model predicts each Henry VI play
   better than the remaining Shakespeare plays do (affinity -0.05 to -0.08 bits/char). But Kyd's
   *Spanish Tragedy* (-0.054) and the anonymous *Taming of a Shrew* (-0.037) show the same affinity.
   So "Marlowe-like" here means "heroic blank-verse history/tragedy of 1587-1592", a register Marlowe
   shaped and everyone imitated. The test cannot tell shared register from shared authorship.

Reading 2 and 3 together: if one person wrote both canons, Marlowe's hand should be visible across
early Shakespeare, not only in the plays that scholars already consider collaborative, and it should
be more visible there than in Kyd. Neither holds.

## What was done

**Corpus.** 7 Marlowe plays, 8 Shakespeare plays dated 1594 or earlier, 11 later Shakespeare plays,
5 early control plays (Kyd, Peele, Lyly, two anonymous), 3 later controls, and 5 plays by Fletcher and
Ford as multi-play "known different author" benchmarks. Dialogue only; speaker labels, stage directions,
act/scene headings and apparatus stripped. Modern spelling throughout (Folger Shakespeare and Folger
EMED regularised texts). Shakespeare and non-Shakespeare come from different Folger products; the
same play in two editions (Faustus, Jew of Malta from Gutenberg) sits at cosine delta 0.01-0.05 from
itself versus about 1.0 between different plays, so edition is not driving results
(`results/*/source_confound.md`).

**Protocol.** Everything is leave-one-play-out, and plays that share plot and characters (Henry VI 1-3
with Richard III; Tamburlaine 1 and 2; the two Shrew plays) are held out together so that a sequel
cannot vouch for its sibling.

**Measurements.**
- Distances, clustering and PCA on z-scored word frequencies, three feature sets: 150 most frequent
  words, 500 most frequent words present in 80% of plays, and a fixed list of about 190 function words.
- Nearest-centroid attribution of each held-out play.
- Logistic-regression chunk classifiers (2000-word chunks) for pairs of play sets, with same-author
  and different-author benchmark pairs.
- Character 6-gram Witten-Bell language models per author, size-matched (equal character budget,
  equal share from every training play), scoring the cross-entropy of each held-out play. The
  `masked` variant additionally replaces proper nouns with a placeholder. Affinity = cross-entropy
  double-centred over plays and models, which removes "this model is generally better" and
  "this play is generally easier".

## Results in more detail

### Clustering (`results/func/dendrogram.png`, `pca.png`)

The first split is not Marlowe versus Shakespeare. It is 1587-92 heroic tragedy/history (all Marlowe
except Faustus and Jew of Malta, Henry VI 1-3, Titus, Kyd, Dido) versus everything else. Richard III,
Errors, Shrew and Two Gentlemen cluster with later Shakespeare and the comedies. Tamburlaine 1 and 2
are each other's nearest neighbour at delta 0.18, four times closer than anything else in the corpus;
1 Henry VI is their next neighbour at 0.61.

### Attribution (`results/func/attribution_loo.md`)

| held-out | nearest author | d(Marlowe) | d(Shakespeare) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 & 2 Tamburlaine, Faustus, Edward II, Massacre | Marlowe | 0.58-0.82 | 1.04-1.19 |
| Dido, Jew of Malta | Kyd | 0.65, 1.00 | 1.12, 1.13 |
| 1, 2 Henry VI | Marlowe | 0.61-0.70 | 0.99-1.12 |
| 3 Henry VI, Titus, Richard III | Kyd | 0.62-0.90 | 0.79-1.10 |
| Two Gentlemen, Errors | Shakespeare | 1.20-1.39 | 0.69-0.77 |
| Shrew | Dekker | 1.19 | 1.01 |
| Spanish Tragedy (Kyd) | Marlowe | 0.65 | 0.99 |

Overall accuracy is 19/39 because single-play control authors are nearly unidentifiable, as expected.
The pattern that matters: comedies are never Marlowe; the history/tragedy plays of 1590-92 are
Marlowe-or-Kyd whoever wrote them.

### Chunk classifiers (`results/func/pairwise_distinguishability.md`, function words)

| pair | type | accuracy | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marlowe vs Shakespeare early, Henry VI excluded | question | 0.85 | 0.92 |
| Marlowe vs Shakespeare early, all 8 | question | 0.67 | 0.76 |
| Marlowe vs Shakespeare later | question | 0.95 | 0.99 |
| Shakespeare early vs later | same author, 5-15 years apart | 0.79 | 0.85 |
| Shakespeare 1595-97 vs 1599-1611 | same author | 0.70 | 0.81 |
| Marlowe 1587-89 vs 1589-93 | same author | 0.56 | 0.59 |
| Shakespeare later vs Fletcher | different, same period | 0.93 | 0.89 |
| Shakespeare early vs Fletcher | different | 0.93 | 0.91 |
| Marlowe vs Fletcher | different | 0.89 | 0.87 |

Caveats: with 7-8 plays a side this test has limited power, and chronological drift within an author
(0.70-0.79) is not far below different-author separation (0.89-0.93). The "all 8" row is dragged down
by the Henry VI plays: when one is held out, its siblings and Richard III leave training too, so the
Shakespeare side is mostly comedies and the history play reads as Marlowe. Pairs with two plays on a
side (Ford) are uninformative and are left in the table only to show that.

### Cross-entropy (`results/crossentropy_masked/crossentropy.md`, `affinity.md`)

Gap in bits/char, Marlowe model minus Shakespeare model, masked variant, averaged over held-out plays:

| held-out group | vs all-Shakespeare model | vs later-Shakespeare model |
|---|---|---|
| Marlowe plays | -0.001 | -0.060 |
| Henry VI 1-3 | +0.001 | -0.010 |
| Titus, R3, Shrew, TGV, Errors | +0.100 | +0.078 |
| Shakespeare later | +0.132 | +0.130 |
| early controls | +0.033 | +0.001 |
| Fletcher | +0.087 | +0.086 |
| Ford | +0.093 | +0.064 |

The Marlowe model has no advantage on any Shakespeare play outside Henry VI, and on those three it
only draws level with the Shakespeare model. Per play, every Marlowe play except Edward II is best
predicted by the Marlowe model; every early Shakespeare play except the Henry VI three is best
predicted by a Shakespeare model; Kyd goes to Marlowe.

## Limits and what would sharpen the answer

- **Register confound.** The 1587-92 history/tragedy register is shared by Marlowe, Kyd and the Henry VI
  plays. The only fix is more same-register, different-author plays from those years: Greene
  (*Alphonsus*, *James IV*), Peele (*Edward I*, *Battle of Alcazar*), Lodge (*Wounds of Civil War*),
  *Arden of Faversham*, *Edward III*. They are not on Folger EMED; Gutenberg has some in old spelling,
  which would need regularising.
- **Small samples.** Seven and eight plays. Scene-level units would multiply the samples and also let
  the test speak to the Henry VI co-authorship claims scene by scene.
- **Corrupt texts.** *Massacre at Paris* and *Doctor Faustus* survive in damaged or revised forms.
- **No neural model yet.** The cross-entropy test is the n-gram version of "train a model per author
  and compare behaviour". Fine-tuning a small pretrained model per author and repeating the held-out
  perplexity comparison is the natural next step and needs PyTorch, which is not installed here.
  Comparing the fine-tuned weights directly would still not be meaningful, for the reasons discussed
  at the outset.

## Reproducing the downloads

```
# Folger Shakespeare (modern spelling)
curl -L https://flgr.sh/txtfss2H6txt -o data/raw/folger/2H6.txt      # codes: 1H6 2H6 3H6 Tit Shr TGV Err MND Rom 1H4 Ham Mac Tmp ...
curl -L https://folger-main-site-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2022/11/richard-iii_TXT_FolgerShakespeare.txt -o data/raw/folger/R3.txt
# Folger EMED regularised XML (level 3)
curl -L https://emed.folger.edu/sites/default/files/folger_encodings/xml/EMED-DrFaust-3.xml -o data/raw/emed/DrFaust.xml
# Gutenberg alternates
curl -L https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/779/pg779.txt -o data/raw/gutenberg/pg779.txt
```
