# Independent review 4 of 4: adversarial, the one-author case (2026-10-31)

Brief: argue the one-author side as strongly as the data allow, then say where it breaks. Files read in full: REPORT.md, README.md, NOTEBOOK.md, results/REPORT_v1_2026-10-30.md, and the func / gi_verify / crossentropy_masked / words / scene_scan / finetune tables on all three corpora. Nothing in the project was modified.

## Part 1. The strongest one-author case the data allow

**The report's own controls show that no method here can identify Marlowe inside his register.** Marlowe's plays verify as Marlowe at 0.47-0.52 and as Peele at 0.50-0.51. Per play it is worse: 1 Tamburlaine verifies as Marlowe 0.09 and as Peele 0.99 (EP 0.05 / 1.00; quarto 0.03 / 0.98). The function-word chunk classifier gives Marlowe vs Peele 0.57-0.60, Marlowe vs Greene 0.57-0.59, Greene vs Peele 0.60. Zeta gives Marlowe vs Peele 0.49-0.54 and Greene vs Peele 0.43-0.44. Character models predict Marlowe's plays best with Peele or Greene models; GPT-2 does the same in 6 of 7 cases. The consequence the report does not draw is that every reported "separation" from Marlowe is therefore a statement that the compared text is outside the heroic register, not a statement about the hand. The case against one author collapses to: Titus, Richard III and three comedies are not heroic plays.

**The genre-matched test exists, was run on all three corpora, and is reported nowhere.** `results/func/pairwise_distinguishability.md` row "Marlowe vs Shakespeare early, histories/Titus only (genre-matched)": accuracy 0.43 / AUC 0.36 (Folger/EMED), 0.38 / 0.30 (EarlyPrint), 0.65 / 0.55 (quarto). The companion row, "Shakespeare early histories/Titus vs early R3+comedies" (same author, same years): 0.66 / 0.78 and 0.71 / 0.82. Neither row appears in REPORT.md, NOTEBOOK.md or README.md. Zeta says the same: "Henry VI 1-3 vs Shakespeare early, Henry VI excluded" separates at 0.80 / 0.90 (EP 0.79 / 0.87), i.e. Shakespeare's own genre split within 1591-94 is as large as the Marlowe-vs-non-H6 split (0.79-0.85) that headline 1 rests on.

**The date-matched Marlowe plays already look like early Shakespeare.** Marlowe's three plays of 1590-93 (Jew of Malta, Edward II, Massacre) are on average nearer Shakespeare's 1591-93 histories and Titus (cosine delta 0.950) than they are to Marlowe's own 1587-89 plays (0.971) on the function-word distance matrix (EP 0.995 vs 0.989, a tie). Edward II's nearest plays in the whole 57-play corpus are Massacre 0.671, 3 Henry VI 0.694, Jew of Malta 0.752. Edward II verifies as Shakespeare at 0.66 (EP 0.47, Q 0.30) and Faustus at 0.52 (EP 0.66, Q 0.48), inside the report's calibrated same-author band of 0.47-0.67; the group mean of 0.22 hides this. On the source-fair GPT-2 run, the Jew of Malta is predicted better by the early-Shakespeare model (5.941 bits/token) than by the Marlowe model trained on the other half of his canon (6.071), a 0.13-bit margin larger than the 0.06-0.11 margins by which the report says Henry VI "goes to Shakespeare"; Faustus ties.

**Titus is a register play, not a Shakespeare play, on most of the evidence.** Impostors: as Marlowe 0.14 / 0.23 / 0.10, as Peele 0.50 / 0.57 / 0.29, as Shakespeare 0.76 / 0.57 / 0.85 (main / EP / Q). On EP it is a dead heat between Shakespeare and Peele. Character models on the 1594 quarto: anonymous-heroic 2.220 and Marlowe 2.229 both beat every Shakespeare model (Sh-later 2.296). Nearest plays: Spanish Tragedy 0.760, 2 Henry VI 0.770, David 0.818, Alcazar 0.853. Chunk classifier P(Marlowe) 0.43 (main), 0.43 (EP), 0.59 (quarto).

**The comedies cannot be tested, and the controls show what a heroic author's comedy looks like under these methods: it looks like Shakespeare.** Peele's Old Wives Tale verifies as Peele at 0.09 / 0.05 / 0.04 and as Shakespeare at 0.71 / 0.84 / 0.69. Greene's James IV: as Greene 0.41 / 0.31 / 0.27, as Shakespeare 0.45 / 0.53 / 0.56. Kyd's Spanish Tragedy as Shakespeare 0.90 / 0.69 / 0.58. So "Shrew, Two Gentlemen, Errors verify as Shakespeare 0.89 and as Marlowe 0.04" is exactly the pattern the method produces for a non-heroic play by a known heroic-register author. Marlowe wrote no surviving comedy; the design cannot in principle reject "same hand, different register."

**The quarto corpus does not replicate as cleanly as the report says.** REPORT.md cites only the GI numbers. On the same corpus the chunk classifier "Marlowe vs Shakespeare early, Henry VI excluded" is 0.64 / 0.62 with Titus at P(Marlowe) 0.59 and Richard III at 0.67 (`results/q/func/pairwise_distinguishability.md`), both on Marlowe's side.

**Chronology.** Marlowe's plays have median date 1589, Shakespeare's early plays 1592. The impostors "Shakespeare" candidate set is 18 plays, 11 of them 1595-1611; the character-model table is explicitly H(Marlowe) − H(later-Shakespeare); the headline "Marlowe vs Shakespeare later 0.95" is a chronology test by construction. The report's own benchmark says a single author drifts to 0.70-0.79 accuracy over 5-15 years.

## Part 2. Objections ranked by how well they survive the project's controls

1. **The Henry VI plays cannot be split from Marlowe, and the register argument proves too much. SURVIVES.** Every method, both printings. GI: H6 as Marlowe 0.55 / 0.41, as anon-heroic 0.53 / 0.56, as Shakespeare 0.38 / 0.29; reported texts (Q) 0.30 / 0.31 / 0.60. The report's rebuttal is that Marlowe is also unattributable within the register; that is true and it means the method is silent, not that it favours Shakespeare. The Cade-scene result survives the prose control but is a register-level statement and also implies the rest of 2H6 is *not* Marlowe-like, which cuts both ways.

2. **The design cannot in principle run a clean genre-matched test. SURVIVES.** The only genre-matched Shakespeare material is one plot family (H6 1-3 + R3) plus Titus. The family rule means that whenever an H6 part is held out, the Shakespeare training side is Titus alone. That is why the genre-matched rows are degenerate and why the same rule cripples the Marlowe 1587-89 vs 1589-93 benchmark. These rows should be reported with that caveat rather than omitted.

3. **Titus belongs to the register, and the Shakespeare call is weak. PARTLY SURVIVES.** On EP it is 0.57 Shakespeare / 0.57 Peele. What fails is the stronger form "Titus is Marlowe's": as Marlowe it is 0.10-0.23, below every other heroic play by another hand (Alcazar 0.81, David 0.81, Wounds 0.72, Edward III 0.80, Jack Straw 0.90). Titus is Peele-ish or anonymous-ish, not Marlowe-ish, and Act 1 goes to Peele at 0.83, consistent with the standard co-authorship view.

4. **Marlowe's late plays already read as early Shakespeare. PARTLY SURVIVES.** Replicates on EP. Against it: the same drift makes Edward II nearer Kyd (0.763) and Arden (0.805) too, i.e. drift toward the 1590-93 London stage, not toward Shakespeare specifically; and Marlowe-to-Peele (0.900) is still closer than Marlowe-to-Sh-heroic (0.966). Reduces to objection 1.

5. **The 0.79 vs 0.85 gap is not meaningful at these play counts. PARTLY SURVIVES.** Chunk-level binomial SE is about 0.03-0.035, so 0.06 is under 2 SE before accounting for play clustering; one or two plays flipping erases the gap. On EP the gap is wider (0.73 vs 0.86). The report dropped from v2 the v1 row "Shakespeare 1595-97 vs 1599-1611: 0.70 / 0.81", the fairer within-career benchmark. The objection fails as an explanation because Marlowe vs Greene/Peele at 0.57-0.60 is itself genre-matched, so "non-H6 early Shakespeare is further from Marlowe than his imitators are" is true and is a genre statement.

6. **The impostors method cannot confirm Marlowe as Marlowe, so its 0.05 for early Shakespeare is uninterpretable. FAILS as stated, survives in weaker form.** Column means over non-members are comparable (as Shakespeare over non-Shakespeare plays 0.35 / 0.29 / 0.25; as Marlowe over non-Marlowe plays 0.32 / 0.30 / 0.30), so the candidate-set asymmetry does not inflate the Shakespeare column on average. The weaker form stands: "as Marlowe" is effectively "near Tamburlaine", so 0.04 for Richard III or Errors means "not a Tamburlaine-style play", nothing finer.

7. **Every reported separation is genre; the comedies are untestable. SURVIVES as a limit, FAILS as positive evidence.** One number cuts against the one-author side: Shakespeare's comedies are further from Marlowe (mean 1.147; TGV 1.404) than Peele's comedy is from Peele's heroic plays (1.066) or Greene's from Greene's (0.80-0.95), but those are two and three data points.

8. **Richard III. FAILS.** As Marlowe 0.04 / 0.01 / 0.04 on all three corpora including the 1597 quarto; nearest plays Errors, Macbeth, Richard II; nearest Marlowe play Edward II at 0.974; Zeta 8% / 0% of chunks Marlowe; char models +0.087 Folio, +0.044 quarto; GPT-2 Sh-later 5.87 vs Marlowe 6.10; scene scan finds nothing Marlowe-like. A one-author believer must make R3 the first post-Marlowe play, which given its 1593 date is possible but is no longer a stylometric argument.

9. **Edition and chronology of the "Shakespeare" definition. FAILS, with one correction.** Headline function-word results are stable across corpora. Correction: source_confound.md shows Edward II EMED vs EarlyPrint at cosine delta 0.107, not the "0.01-0.05" claimed, and the cross-source pairs on the main corpus all fall on EP (Marlowe vs anon 0.75 → 0.63; Sh early vs Greene 0.70 → 0.64; vs Peele 0.81 → 0.72; vs anon 0.65 → 0.58), so function words are not immune to edition either.

10. **What changed v1 → v2. Mostly FAILS as an objection; two omissions are real.** Retracting "Marlowe's plays recognise each other" was correct. Dropping the all-8 row (0.67 / 0.76) and the within-later-career row (0.70 / 0.81) from the v2 table, and never reporting the genre-matched (0.43 / 0.38) and within-Shakespeare-genre (0.66 / 0.71) rows, all move in the same direction: the table a reader sees understates how much of the 0.85 is genre and period.

## Part 3. Analyses demanded before accepting the conclusion

1. **Hero and Leander (1593) against Venus and Adonis (1593) and Lucrece (1594), with Chapman's 1598 continuation as the built-in different-hand control** and other epyllia / complaints of 1589-98 as impostors (Lodge's Scilla, Daniel's Rosamond, Drayton, Marston's Pigmalion). Same genre, same two years, no company, no collaboration, no reported texts, no Folio. If Venus verifies with Hero as Chapman's part does not, objection 7 is dead; if it does not, the project's conclusion is probably right for the wrong reasons.

2. **A classifier trained only on Marlowe vs Greene + Peele + Kyd + Lodge + anonymous heroic (never on any Shakespeare play), using scene-level units and word-adjacency networks or any feature set that first passes Greene-vs-Peele at or above 0.75**, then applied to H6 1-3, Titus, R3 and Edward III scene by scene. Until a feature set clears the Greene/Peele acceptance test, the genre-matched question is unanswerable and the report should say that the Henry VI / Titus verdict is "no evidence either way".

One-line verdict: the report's conclusion holds for Richard III and holds as "cannot be tested" for the comedies; it does not hold for Henry VI or Titus, and the omitted genre-matched rows show that the project knew where the weakness was.
