# Independent review 3 of 4: domain and scholarship (2026-10-31)

Brief: assess corpus and canon choices, editions, methods relative to the attribution literature, conclusions versus published scholarship, and what the field would consider missing. Literature facts were checked against web sources where they matter.

## Verdict
The report's negative conclusion ("the evidence does not support a single author") is defensible only as an absence-of-evidence statement, and its stronger headline, that non-Henry VI early Shakespeare is "further from Marlowe than Marlowe's own imitators", is not: that comparison pits seven heroic verse plays against three prose-heavy comedies plus two tragedies, is matched in size by the project's own same-author contrasts (Shakespeare early histories vs early comedies 0.66-0.71 / AUC 0.78-0.82; early vs later Shakespeare 0.73-0.79), and the one genre-matched comparison in the project's own tables (Marlowe vs Shakespeare's early histories+Titus, accuracy 0.43 main / 0.38 EP) is omitted from REPORT.md. The methods cannot recognise Marlowe's own plays (1 Tamburlaine verifies as Marlowe at 0.09 and as Peele at 0.99; nearest-centroid attribution gets 2 of 7 Marlowe plays right), so their silence about Marlowe in Titus, Richard III or the comedies carries little weight in either direction. The correct domain-level conclusion is "these tools at this corpus size do not have the power to test the hypothesis, and the single test that does separate the canons is confounded by genre and editorial lineage", which sits comfortably with the published literature (Craig 2009; Burrows & Craig 2017; Barber 2021; Rizvi 2019).

## Findings, ranked

**1. The headline comparison is genre-confounded, and the genre-matched test that contradicts it is left out of the report. Undermines conclusion 1.**
`results/func/pairwise_distinguishability.md` row "Marlowe vs Shakespeare early, histories/Titus only (genre-matched)": acc 0.43, AUC 0.36 (EP: 0.38 / 0.30). Per play in the headline test, P(Marlowe): Tit 0.43, R3 0.35, Shr 0.34, TGV 0.04, Err 0.08; the separation is carried by the comedies. The per-word table confirms the mechanism (Shakespeare side: you, her, say, very, indeed, marry, fool, patience, tongue; Marlowe side: shall, die, live, pride, walls, perish); the report says so for Zeta but does not apply the same reasoning to the function-word result. Caveat: the genre-matched row is itself nearly powerless because the Henry VI family rule leaves Titus alone on the Shakespeare side whenever a Henry VI part is held out. The correct statement is "no adequately powered genre-matched test has been run". Marlowe wrote no comedy; any method will put comic prose on the non-Marlowe side.

**2. The methods fail their own positive controls on Marlowe, which voids the "calibrated" readings of Titus and R3. Undermines the impostors argument.**
`results/gi_verify/gi_verify.md` per play: 1Tam 0.09 as Marlowe / 0.99 as Peele; 2Tam 0.24 / 0.99; Dido 0.42 / 0.64; Locrine 0.95 Peele; Wounds 0.92 Peele; TRKJ 0.89 Peele. `results/func/attribution_loo.md`: 2/7 Marlowe plays attributed to Marlowe. Design problems in gi_verify.py: (a) candidate-set size is asymmetric (Shakespeare 18 vs Marlowe 6, Greene/Peele 4); (b) impostors are drawn from every period and genre, whereas Koppel & Winter 2014 and Kestemont et al. 2016 require genre-matched impostors; (c) when the candidate is Marlowe, the target's own other Shakespeare plays serve as impostors, so "Titus is not Marlowe" reduces to "Titus's nearest neighbour is another Shakespeare play", true under both hypotheses. Kestemont et al. use min-max distance, not cosine; acceptable but should be stated as a deviation.

**3. The "single-pipeline" EarlyPrint corpus carries author-correlated editorial artefacts. Weakens.**
(a) *ay*: EarlyPrint regularises "I" (yes) to "ay" only in the EMED-lineage texts: every Marlowe play (0.17-2.26 per 1,000), Kyd, Lyly, Leir, Soliman, Arden, TTR3, and in none of the Shakespeare texts, Folio or quarto (0-0.2 per 1,000). *ay* is in the project's function-word list and is the top EP Zeta Marlowe marker. In the EP and quarto corpora "ay" = "EMED-curated text" = "not Shakespeare". *straight* (EP d = +6.5) and *whether/whither* behave similarly. (b) Proper-noun masking is edition-dependent (Folio 7.5-8.9% masked vs 3.3-4.5% for quartos). (c) Clitic tokenisation differs by lineage ("I'll" one token in Folger; "I 'll" / "'t is" split in EMED and EarlyPrint), inflating *i, we, you, is* and creating *ll/tis/twas* tokens that appear in Zeta marker lists. (d) The report says the edition effect on word features is "0.01-0.05 cosine delta"; `results/func/source_confound.md` gives 0.008-0.107 (Ed2 EMED vs EarlyPrint 0.107); the most edition-sensitive words are *whether, ay, tis, there, i*, and *whether* is the project's #2 Marlowe-vs-Shakespeare discriminator (d = 2.9). Fix: drop or merge *ay/i, tis, ll, em, 's, whether/whither*; mask names from a fixed list; re-run EP and Q.

**4. Chunk sizes and set sizes make the acceptance test an under-powered standard. Weakens conclusion 2.**
Greene vs Peele is 4 plays a side, ~55k words, 27 chunks a side: 0.60/0.71 is what a weak but real signal looks like at that n. Eder 2015 puts the reliable floor at ~2,500-5,000 words per sample; the 2,000-word chunk is marginal and the 100-1,400-word scenes are below it (2H6 4.3 = 132 words, 4.5 = 102, 4.6 = 117 are reported as if meaningful). Craig 2009 validated on 2,000-word segments but against 27 Shakespeare plays and a 109-play counter-set. Barber 2021 (DSH 36.3), replicating Burrows & Craig with matched canon sizes, found Zeta "struggles to tell Marlowe from Shakespeare": the project's register finding is a known small-canon limitation, not a discovery. Greene's set is half romantic comedy and Peele's includes a biblical play and a folk comedy, so "same register" is loose on both sides.

**5. Corpus and canon choices. Weakens (mostly fixable).**
- Marlowe: Dido is safe (Freebury-Jones & Dahl 2020; Lunney & Craig 2020: no Nashe). Faustus A-text includes comic scenes most editors give to a collaborator. Massacre is by consensus a memorial reconstruction and borrows from 3H6 itself. Jew of Malta 1633 acceptable. No sensitivity run without MP and the Faustus comic scenes; there should be one. Date inconsistency: works.json dates Faustus 1589, EMED/EarlyPrint headers say 1592.
- Shakespeare early: NOS (Taylor & Loughnane 2017) credits 1H6 to "Marlowe, Nashe and Anonymous, adapted by Shakespeare", 2H6/3H6 to "Shakespeare, Marlowe and another, revised by Shakespeare"; Titus to Shakespeare and Peele (Act 1, 2.1, 2.2, 4.1); best-guess dates TGV 1588, Titus 1589, 2H6/3H6 1590, Shrew 1591, R3 1592, Errors 1594. Titus is kept intact (Peele's Act 1 is 3,748 of 19.8k words). The scene scan's Titus-with-Peele result (P(Peele)=0.83 for 1.1, 0.70 for 2.4) is its best-behaved output and is under-used.
- 2H6/3H6 quarto handling: A68931 is Contention Q2 (1600) and A10726 the 1595 octavo; correctly identified and flagged as reported texts; family rule correctly applied.
- Greene/Peele: Alcazar Q1594 and Orlando Q1594 are abridged/reported texts (Greg); Edward I "very corrupt"; OWT is 7.6k words. Both control canons are textually worse than Shakespeare's.
- "Anonymous heroic" is not an author and should not be a GI candidate. It contains Soliman (Kyd by consensus), King Leir (Kyd per Vickers/Freebury-Jones), Arden (NOS: Shakespeare scenes 4-9), Edward III (NOS: Shakespeare in the Countess scenes and sc. 12), Selimus and Locrine (Greene-circle), TRKJ (Peele per Vickers 2004), Jack Straw (Peele proposed), TTR3 (no consensus). Edward III and Arden belong in a "Shakespeare-partial" positive-control group; "Henry VI verifies as anonymous-heroic 0.53-0.60" is uninterpretable as it stands.
- TCP ids check out. A68114 Selimus is the 1638 reissue of the 1594 sheets; fine. A68278 is Part 1 of Troublesome Reign only (13.4k words) and should be labelled.

**6. Text cleaning. Minor.**
No wrong texts, no surviving speaker labels or stage directions in spot-checks. Both Tamburlaine prologues are dropped from the EMED corpus (present in EP); main 1Tam ends with two stray punctuation lines; TCP gap markers survive in TRKJ, R3 Q, 3H6 octavo, LLL (harmless for word features); TTR3 is largely printed as prose in Q1594, which matters for any verse-only or metrical work.

**7. The 2 Henry VI / Craig & Kinney claim is overstated; Taylor/Loughnane are sidestepped. Weakens conclusion 3.**
Craig 2009 found Marlowe "strong in IV.iii-ix" and explicitly "not extend[ing] to IV.ii"; the project labels 4.2-4.10 "Marlowe per Craig & Kinney" and scores 4.2 at P(Marlowe)=0.48. The scan's P(Marlowe) 0.56 vs 0.23 and ΔH +0.216 vs +0.243 (both strongly Shakespeare-ward, difference 0.027 bits/char) is directional agreement at best; the verse-only check rests on 2,041 verse words. Nothing is said about Craig's 1H6 finding (Marlowe in the Joan sequence, Act 3) or Burrows & Craig 2017 (3H6: non-Shakespeare segments fall to Marlowe). Segarra et al. 2016 put Act 1 of 2H6 with Marlowe; Eisen et al. 2018 address the Marlovian theory directly. Critiques the project's own results echo (Freebury-Jones 2016; Freebury-Jones & Dahl 2018; Rizvi 2018/2019/2023; Barber 2020/2021; Vickers 2007/2017) are not cited. Nashe and Kyd are absent as candidates although they are the live alternatives.

**8. Zeta: implementation is in line with Craig/Hoover practice; the genre finding is already in the literature. Cosmetic.**
Burrows 2007; Hoover 2010 shows Zeta markers are low-frequency content words; Schöch et al. 2018 treat it as usable for genre; Rizvi 2019 argues bisector separation "is not a research finding". Cite these rather than present the finding as new.

**9. Task-vector experiment. Cosmetic.** Correctly run and correctly interpreted as uninformative.

## What to try next, ranked (with the result that would change the conclusion)
1. **Genre-matched Marlowe/Shakespeare test with power.** Marlowe (7) vs Shakespeare's histories/tragedies 1590-97 (Tit, R3, R2, Rom, 1H4, JC, with Henry VI as a separate held-out set), alongside Marlowe vs Greene-heroic (Alphonsus, Orlando) and Marlowe vs Peele-heroic (Alcazar, Edward I, David), and Shakespeare-history vs Shakespeare-comedy of the same years as the same-author yardstick. Changes the conclusion if genre-matched Marlowe-vs-Shakespeare separates no better than Shakespeare-history-vs-Shakespeare-comedy (headline 1 must go), or if it clearly exceeds that yardstick and Marlowe-vs-Peele (headline 1 is earned).
2. **Non-dramatic verse.** Hero and Leander (1593) vs Venus and Adonis (1593) and Lucrece (1594), with Chapman's 1598 continuation as the built-in different-author control inside one poem, and Lodge, Heywood, Marston, Drayton as same-genre impostors; Ovid's Elegies/Lucan vs the Sonnets as a secondary rhymed test. Removes collaboration, reporting, theatrical genre and prose at once.
3. **Verse-form features** (Tarlinskaja 2014; Oras 1960): feminine endings, run-on lines, mid-line pause position, rhyme rate, per play and per rolling window, checked against Tarlinskaja's published figures (Tamburlaine ~2.5% feminine endings vs Comedy of Errors 13.5%). Insensitive to spelling regularisation. Changes the conclusion if Titus/R3/Edward III-Countess profiles fall inside Marlowe's 1590-93 range once dated, or outside it while Edward II is inside.
4. **Fix the EP/Q artefacts and re-run**: drop/merge ay/i, tis, ll, em, 's, whether/whither; edition-neutral name masking; report masking rates per play.
5. **Shakespeare-partial positive controls**: Edward III (Countess scenes) and Arden (scenes 4-9) scored scene-by-scene. If the method cannot see Shakespeare in the Countess scenes, its silence on Marlowe in Henry VI is formally uninformative and conclusion 3 should be withdrawn.
6. **Rolling attribution instead of scene cuts** (Eder 2016), 2,000-word windows at 500-word steps, with Nashe (Summer's Last Will) and Kyd (ST, Soliman, Cornelia) added as candidates, and a permutation test of overlap with Craig 2009, Burrows & Craig 2017 and NOS scene lists.
7. **Power calibration of the acceptance test**: downsample known-different pairs (Shakespeare-early vs Fletcher, Shakespeare-late vs Middleton) to 4 plays × ~55k words and run the identical pipeline. If those also come out near 0.6, the Greene-vs-Peele standard measures n, not method.
8. **Word-adjacency networks** (Segarra 2016; Eisen 2018) and word bigram/collocation tests, read against Rizvi 2018/2023 and Barber 2020.
9. **Standard-form impostors** (Kestemont et al. 2016): equal candidate-set sizes, same-period/genre impostor pool, min-max distance, per-play scores with bootstrap intervals; bootstrap consensus tree over MFW 100-1,000 (Eder 2013).
10. **Sensitivity runs on the Marlowe canon**: without Massacre; Faustus with comic scenes removed; Dido in/out; Jew in/out.
