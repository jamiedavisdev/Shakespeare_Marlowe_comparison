# Independent review 1 of 4: code audit (2026-10-31)

Brief: audit every script for leakage, statistical correctness, corpus construction and report/result agreement; re-run what is cheap. Reviewer re-ran word_profiles.py (main, ep), gi_verify.py (main) and stylometry.py func (main): all regenerated byte-identical outputs. finetune.py not run. Nothing in the project was modified.

## Confirmed bugs and discrepancies, most serious first

### 1. Proper-noun masking is capitalisation-driven, so the `~` placeholder rate is an edition signature. Most of the reported Folio-vs-quarto effect is this artefact. (High confidence, measured)
- `scripts/crossentropy.py:39-48` (`proper_nouns`), same rule in `scene_scan.py:72-79`, `quarto_check.py:9-11`, `edition_effect.py:13-14`. A word is masked if capitalised in >=90% of its non-line-initial uses *within that play*. The 1623 Folio capitalises common nouns (King, Lord, Queen, Crown, Father...), early quartos and EarlyPrint Marlowe/Greene/Peele mostly do not.
- Measured share of tokens replaced by `~`: EP corpus Henry VI 1-3 and R3 7.5-8.9%, Shakespeare-early mean 5.5%, Shakespeare-later 4.4%, vs Marlowe 4.1%, Greene 4.1%, Peele 3.9%. Quarto corpus: 2H6/3H6 4.6-5.2%, R3 3.3%, later quartos 2.7%. Main corpus: Folger Shakespeare 2.3-3.2% vs EMED Marlowe 4.4%, Greene/Peele 4.1%. So in every corpus the Shakespeare side differs from the controls in `~` rate, in a different direction each time.
- Direct test (same models, family exclusion, masked vs unmasked): Folio->quarto change in H(Marlowe)-H(Sh-early) for R3 is -0.056 masked but -0.011 unmasked; for Titus -0.026 masked, -0.016 unmasked. About 80% of the R3 "quarto sits nearer Marlowe" effect and ~40% of the Titus one is masking, not printing.
- Affects: REPORT "Corpora" paragraph ("1594 Titus and 1597 Richard III quartos sit 0.03-0.06 bits/char nearer the Marlowe model"), `results/ep/crossentropy_masked/quarto_check.md`, NOTEBOOK step 9, the rationale for building corpus `q`, and every masked cross-entropy / affinity number at the 0.01-0.05 bits/char level, which is the size of the affinities being interpreted. The report's bottom-line character-model gap (+0.07 for non-H6 early Shakespeare under Marlowe vs -0.04/-0.06 for Greene/Peele) is also affected but not reversed: the unmasked EP gap for Titus vs Sh-early is -0.010, R3 +0.009.

### 2. Zeta scoring: in-sample centroids after in-sample marker selection; held-out numbers are dominated by fold-to-fold centroid drift. The same-author AUC of 0.26/0.23 is the giveaway. (High confidence, measured)
- `scripts/word_profiles.py:213-221`. Markers (150 a side) are chosen on the training chunks, then the centroids are the means of *those same* training chunks; in-sample accuracy is 1.00 for every pair (total overfit, ~50 chunks vs 300 selected features). Each held-out chunk is then placed by nearest centroid, and the AUC margin subtracts a fold-specific midpoint before pooling across folds.
- Measured on main corpus: for "Marlowe 1587-89 vs 1589-93" the mean held-out margin is -0.161 for A chunks and -0.108 for B chunks (everything lands on the B side because when 1Tam or 2Tam is held out the family rule leaves Dido alone as A), which produces acc 0.54 / AUC 0.26. Pooled AUC on the raw held-out score sA-sB (no centroid subtraction) is 0.78 for that pair, 0.88 for the Shakespeare halves, 0.57 Greene vs Peele, 0.60 Marlowe vs Peele, 0.93 Marlowe vs Greene, 0.91 Marlowe vs non-H6 early Shakespeare.
- Affects: the whole Zeta table in REPORT (both corpora), most for 3-4-play sets. "Zeta fails the acceptance test (0.43-0.44, below chance)" is a measurement artefact; the raw-score AUC of 0.57 is still weak, so the qualitative verdict may survive, but the numbers as reported cannot be read as separability. An AUC below 0.5 for a same-author benchmark should have been flagged in the report rather than tabulated.

### 3. "Size-matched" character models and "identical token budgets" are not. (Confirmed)
- `scripts/crossentropy.py:115-118`: `TEXT[w][:share]` cannot take more than a play has, and short plays are not compensated. Measured (EP, masked): Greene model 244,632 of 273,328 chars (89%) and Peele 229,440 (84%) even when scoring other authors; when one of their own plays is held out, 186-224k (Greene) and 168-222k (Peele), i.e. 61-82% of budget, while the Marlowe, Shakespeare and Anon models sit at 100%. Same pattern in `scene_scan.py:138-141` and `finetune.py:41-45`: fine-tune budgets actually used are Marlowe_A 91%, Marlowe_B 90%, Greene 91%, Peele 84% (both corpora); REPORT says "identical token budgets (78,818 tokens each)".
- Effect on the claim "Greene's and Peele's plays are predicted better by the Marlowe model than by their own authors' models": re-test with Marlowe cut to exactly the own-model size shows the Marlowe model still wins 6 of 8 (Alphonsus 2.244 vs 2.269, Alcazar 2.243 vs 2.302, David 2.242 vs 2.284; JamesIV and EdwardI go the other way or tie), but the margins shrink from 0.07-0.13 to 0.01-0.06 bits/char. Direction of the published bias: against Greene and Peele everywhere, so "Marlowe's plays are best predicted by Peele" is if anything understated.

### 4. Numbers in REPORT.md that do not match `results/` (Confirmed)
- Character-model table row "Shrew, Two Gentlemen, Errors (Folio only) | +0.05 to +0.06": `results/ep/crossentropy_masked/crossentropy.md` gives H(Marlowe)-H(Sh-later) = +0.108, +0.097, +0.106 (vs Sh-early: +0.107, +0.091, +0.085). The report understates the gap by half, in the direction of making those plays look more Marlowe-like.
- Scene scan: "Act 1 (usually given to Peele) is scored 0.18 Peele by function words, which is too weak to confirm or deny". `results/scene_scan/scene_scan.md` gives Titus 1.1 P(Peele) = 0.83 (P(Shakespeare) 0.13), and dH(Peele) +0.029, the lowest in the play (other scenes +0.11 to +0.20); 2.1 is 0.37 and 2.4 0.70. The results support the Peele attribution more strongly than the report says.
- Bottom line: "calibrated ... different-author scores of 0.05-0.35". `results/gi_verify/gi_verify.md` calibration: Peele plays as Marlowe 0.52, Anon-heroic as Marlowe 0.60, Marlowe as Peele 0.51, Kyd as Shakespeare 0.90 (EP 0.69, q 0.58). The stated range is only true for cross-register pairs.
- Zeta projection: the report quotes main-corpus figures only ("1 Henry VI 78%, 3 Henry VI 64% on Marlowe's side"). On EP (`results/ep/words/zeta.md`) 3H6 is 20% Marlowe-side (i.e. Shakespeare), 1H6 67%, TRKJ 83% not 100%. The replication does not reproduce the 3H6 claim.
- Everything else checked matches: all chunk-classifier rows (func, ep/func, q), all GI group means on all three corpora, all Zeta acc/AUC rows, Marlowe/Greene/Peele/Titus/R3/H6/later cross-entropy gaps, scene-scan and prose/verse summaries, all task-vector cosines and the EP perplexity table.

### 5. GI "as Shakespeare" scores are inflated by Shakespeare having 18-19 candidate plays. (Confirmed by experiment; design issue)
- `scripts/gi_verify.py:48,56`: the candidate distance is the minimum over *all* plays by the candidate author; Shakespeare offers 16-19 candidates, Marlowe 5-6, Greene/Peele 3-4. Minimum distance falls with candidate count. Capping candidates at 4 per round (same 200 rounds, seed 0): non-H6 early Shakespeare "as Shakespeare" 0.86 -> 0.52, later Shakespeare 0.97 -> 0.75, Marlowe "as Marlowe" 0.50 -> 0.42, Peele "as Peele" unchanged 0.66-0.67. "As Marlowe" for Shakespeare plays stays 0.04-0.06, so the Marlowe-side conclusion is robust; the headline "0.86-0.89 as Shakespeare" and column-to-column comparisons are not. Minor: z-scores (`line 42`) are fitted with the target included (standard in GI, negligible).

### 6. The word-level name mask removes common nouns and a function word, differently per corpus. (Confirmed)
- `scripts/word_profiles.py:72-77`: union of per-play (>=90% capitalised) and corpus-wide (>=60%) rules. Main corpus masks 1,691 types including lord (3,526 uses), king, sir, o, god, ay (a word in the project's own function-word list), master, grace, queen, crown, arms, noble, fortune, earth, nature, majesty, wherefore. EP masks 2,151 types including, additionally, son, father, world, heaven, sword, war, brother, house, villain, hell, state. So "names masked" is really "names plus ~40 of the commonest nouns, a different set on each corpus"; e.g. *sword* and *war* appear as Marlowe markers on main but are ineligible on EP. Affects the marker lists and word-profile tables quoted in REPORT more than the accuracies.

### 7. Folger cleaner leaves speaker labels that carry a bracketed direction. (Confirmed, small)
- `scripts/build_corpus.py:79` `SPEAKER_RE` requires the label to end in `[A-Z0-9.]`; "TRANIO, [aside]" becomes "TRANIO," after bracket removal and is kept as dialogue. Same regex in `scene_scan.py:46` and `prose_verse.py:45-46`. 11-125 leaked tokens per Folger play (Err 0.86%, Shr 0.55%, R3 0.37%, 3H6 0.35%, 1H6 0.32%; ~700 lines over 19 plays). They are names, so function-word features ignore them and the masks catch most; in `prose_verse.py` such lines are appended to the previous speech, which can mis-assign a speech's mode. Numeric effect on reported figures: negligible. EP and quarto corpora are clean.

### 8. EMED parser drops dialogue outside `<sp>`; the notebook's parser fix was applied to EarlyPrint only. (Confirmed, small)
- `scripts/build_corpus.py:64-72` iterates `<sp>` only. Dropped: JoM 357 words (the Machevill prologue), Perkin 267, ShoeHol 270, Prize 250, Gal 181, 1Tam/2Tam 61 each (prologues), ST 27. The EP corpus keeps these (JoM 18,172 vs 17,956 words), a small main-vs-EP asymmetry (<=2% of a play).

### 9. Budget truncation takes the *opening* of each play, not a sample. (Confirmed, minor)
- `crossentropy.py:117`, `finetune.py:44`, `scene_scan.py:141`: `[:share]`. The Sh-later character model is the first ~25k characters (roughly Act 1) of each of 11 plays. Not wrong, but "equal share" means "openings only", which is stylistically non-random.

## Suspicions, not confirmed
- Fine-tune behaviour test: GPT-2's pretraining almost certainly contains Shakespeare, so "Henry VI and Richard III predicted best by Shakespeare models" may partly be pretraining familiarity. Base-model bits per token (6.26-6.43 for Shakespeare vs 6.16-6.52 for Marlowe) do not show obvious memorisation; untestable here.
- Pooled-across-folds AUC (stylometry, Zeta) mixes fold calibrations; standard caveat only.
- Chunk-classifier pairs where the family rule leaves one play on a side show the same LOO pessimism as Zeta (SE_a vs Marlowe 0.43/0.36; H6+Titus vs Greene on EP 0.42/0.42). These are not in REPORT, but the "Marlowe vs Henry VI 1-3" Zeta row, which keeps sibling parts in training, is, and is flagged there.
- JoM prose: EMED line markup yields only 124 prose words for the Jew of Malta (Ithamore/Bellamira scenes are prose in modern editions). A data-labelling limit of the verse-only check, not a code bug.

## Areas that are sound
- `stylometry.py` `pair_test` (lines 241-270): vocabulary, scaler and classifier all fitted inside the fold; families held out together; no chunk of a held-out play ever in training; balanced class weights; accuracy and AUC pooled per chunk. Sound.
- `scene_scan.py` Titus leak fix verified (lines 126-131). `prose_verse.py` excludes the Henry VI family from the Shakespeare side (line 84). Mode split sanity-checked on Faustus and 2H6 4.2.
- `finetune.py`: no held-out play is in its scorer's training set; Marlowe plays are scored by the half-canon model that did not see them; cosines over non-embedding parameters are computed correctly.
- `ce_report.py` double-centring is correct. Cohen's d uses pooled SD with ddof=1; fine for 4-play groups given the report's caveat.
- Corpus selection: all three manifests agree on word counts within ~1% per play; Folio `select` heads pick the right plays; no dramatis-personae or front-matter leakage found in EP/quarto texts.

## Verdict
The function-word chunk classifier and the Marlowe-side GI scores (0.04-0.07 "as Marlowe" for non-Henry VI early Shakespeare, on all three corpora) are correctly computed and can be trusted as stated. The GI "as Shakespeare" magnitudes and the calibration ranges cannot be compared across columns as the report does. Every character-model number carries a capitalisation-masking artefact of the same size as the effects being read, and the Folio-vs-quarto finding is mostly that artefact. The Zeta table should not be cited in its current form. The fine-tune weight-delta conclusion stands; its "identical budgets" statement does not.

Re-run / re-implement: (1) the masked character models, `quarto_check.py`, `edition_effect.py` and the scene scan with a capitalisation-independent mask (a fixed name list per play, applied identically to every edition, or no masking plus the family rule); (2) Zeta with a leak-free scorer (fixed threshold or logistic regression on sA-sB, markers still chosen in-fold) and report raw-score AUC; (3) budget allocation in `crossentropy.py`, `scene_scan.py`, `finetune.py` that redistributes the shortfall from short plays; (4) GI with a fixed number of candidate plays per round; (5) correct the four REPORT rows listed under item 4, and either strip "TRANIO,"-style labels (`SPEAKER_RE` should allow a trailing comma) or note the leak.
